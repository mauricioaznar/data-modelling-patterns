Card 3 · V1 Ch 2 · Postal addresses, facilities and geography (Figs 2.8, 2.11)<br><br>Which areas is this address inside, and which physical places do parties use?	<b>Hook:</b> the map and the floor plan<br><b>Mantra:</b> An address lies in many boundaries, boundaries in many boundaries; a facility nests in one, hosts roles, has mechanisms: a place isn't an address.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>postal_address</code> (only the street part: address lines and directions; a contact-mechanism subtype since Fig 2.10, so parties reach it through <code>party_contact_mechanism</code>) · <code>geographic_boundary</code> (city, state, postal code, country, sales territory… as <code>geographic_boundary_type</code> rows; country lives here, so citizenship points at it) linked to addresses by <code>postal_address_boundary</code> · <code>geographic_boundary_association</code> (a boundary within another, many-to-many) · <code>facility</code> (a physical place: warehouse, building, floor, room as <code>facility_type</code> rows, with square footage and one <code>part_of_facility_id</code> parent) · <code>facility_role</code> (a party owns, rents, uses or manages it) and <code>facility_contact_mechanism</code> (its own address, phone…), both dated by us.<br><br><b>W:</b> <code>city</code>, <code>state</code>, <code>zip</code>, <code>country</code> columns on the address: no place for a sales territory, no "everything inside the Midwest" query, and a second country list for citizenship.

Card 1 · V1 Ch 2 · Parties, roles and relationships (Figs 2.1–2.7)<br><br>Who is this, what are they to us, and how are they tied to each other?	<b>Hook:</b> people wearing hats<br><b>Mantra:</b> A party is who; a role is the hat it wears; a relationship joins two hats, and a type names each hat; a class says what it is underneath.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>party</code> (person or organization: one supertype, a <code>party_kind</code> discriminator) · <code>party_role</code> (a party plays a <code>role_type</code>, dated) · <code>party_relationship</code> (from party role → to party role, of a <code>party_relationship_type</code> that says which role types it joins) · <code>party_classification</code> (what a party <i>is</i>, e.g. an industry or a size band, vs. what it <i>does</i> for us).<br><br><b>W:</b> the same company is a customer, a supplier and a partner at once. Naive model: separate customer and supplier tables, so the name and address are duplicated, drift, and "everyone we deal with" needs a union.<br><br><b>T:</b> the relationship joins <i>roles</i>, not parties; and the specific tables (employment, customer relationship, rollup) vs. the one generic <code>party_relationship</code> (your open gap: specific vs generic).<br><br><b>D:</b> party ← party_role → role_type; party_relationship → party_role twice; party_relationship → party_relationship_type → role_type twice.	v1::ch02	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch02-2.1-2.7
Card 2 · V1 Ch 2 · Contact mechanisms (Figs 2.9–2.10)<br><br>How do we reach this party, for what, and since when?	<b>Hook:</b> the switchboard<br><b>Mantra:</b> Postal, phone and e-mail are one mechanism; the party's link to it owns the dates, the purposes and the "don't call"; mechanisms forward to mechanisms.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>contact_mechanism</code> (one supertype for postal address, telecom number, e-mail) · <code>party_contact_mechanism</code> (this party uses this mechanism, dated, with the non-solicitation flag, extension and optional role) · <code>party_contact_mechanism_purpose</code> (why we use that link: billing, shipping, home…, each dated) · <code>contact_mechanism_link</code> (one mechanism tied to another, e.g. call forwarding).<br><br><b>W:</b> columns like <code>home_phone</code>, <code>work_phone</code>, <code>fax</code> on the party: a fixed number of slots, no history, a shared switchboard copied onto every person, no place to say "don't call".<br><br><b>T:</b> the purpose and the do-not-solicit flag belong to the <b>link</b> (party + mechanism), not to the mechanism or the party. The same number can be fine to call for one party and off-limits for another.<br><br><b>D:</b> party ← party_contact_mechanism → contact_mechanism (→ postal / telecom / electronic); party_contact_mechanism ← purpose → purpose_type; contact_mechanism_link → contact_mechanism twice.	v1::ch02	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch02-2.9-2.10
Card 3 · V1 Ch 2 · Postal addresses, facilities and geography (Figs 2.8, 2.11)<br><br>Which areas is this address inside, and which physical places do parties use?	<b>Hook:</b> the map and the floor plan<br><b>Mantra:</b> An address lies in many boundaries, boundaries in many boundaries; a facility nests in one, hosts roles, has mechanisms: a place isn't an address.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>postal_address</code> (only the street part: address lines and directions; a contact-mechanism subtype since Fig 2.10, so parties reach it through <code>party_contact_mechanism</code>) · <code>geographic_boundary</code> (city, state, postal code, country, sales territory… as <code>geographic_boundary_type</code> rows; country lives here, so citizenship points at it) linked to addresses by <code>postal_address_boundary</code> · <code>geographic_boundary_association</code> (a boundary within another, many-to-many) · <code>facility</code> (a physical place: warehouse, building, floor, room as <code>facility_type</code> rows, with square footage and one <code>part_of_facility_id</code> parent) · <code>facility_role</code> (a party owns, rents, uses or manages it) and <code>facility_contact_mechanism</code> (its own address, phone…), both dated by us.<br><br><b>W:</b> <code>city</code>, <code>state</code>, <code>zip</code>, <code>country</code> columns on the address: no place for a sales territory, no "everything inside the Midwest" query, and a second country list for citizenship. And a warehouse modelled as its address: a campus has several buildings at one address, a warehouse can have a mailing and a dock address, a room has a phone and no address, and there's nowhere to put square footage or who leases it.<br><br><b>T:</b> facilities nest by <b>containment</b> (room in floor in building), not by size; a warehouse's two addresses is a facility ↔ mechanism question, not a legal boundary (your first answers to item 49). Don't swap the two nestings: boundaries are many-to-many (a postal code crosses city lines), facilities have one parent. Geography is still unpractised; expect this card to be shaky.<br><br><b>D:</b> contact_mechanism ← postal_address (shared key); postal_address ← postal_address_boundary → geographic_boundary → type; geographic_boundary_association → geographic_boundary twice (from within, to in); facility → facility (part of), → type; party ← facility_role → facility, → role type; facility ← facility_contact_mechanism → contact_mechanism. Ch 3 reuses both: reorder guidelines per facility or boundary, inventory in a facility.	v1::ch02	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch02-2.8_2.11
Card 4 · V1 Ch 2 · Communication events and case (Figs 2.7, 2.12–2.13)<br><br>Who talked to whom, how, about what, and what happened next?	<b>Hook:</b> the meeting minutes<br><b>Mantra:</b> An event happens via a medium, with many parties in roles, for purposes; a case groups past events, a work effort is the work they trigger.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>communication_event</code> (one contact, occurs via a medium, has a status, optionally in the context of a party relationship) · <code>communication_event_role</code> (any number of parties take part, each in a role: caller, receiver, attendee…) · <code>communication_event_purpose</code> (what it was about) · <code>communication_case</code> (looking back: groups the events about one issue) · <code>work_effort</code> via <code>communication_event_work_effort</code> (looking forward: the work an event triggers, many-to-many).<br><br><b>W:</b> an event tied to exactly one relationship (Fig 2.7) can't hold a three-way meeting or a call with someone we have no relationship with yet. A case alone can't say what work came out of a call.<br><br><b>T:</b> case vs work effort: the case <b>groups past events</b>; the work effort is <b>future work they cause</b> (your open gap). Also <code>valid_contact_mechanism_role</code>: a rule stored as rows.<br><br><b>D:</b> communication_event ← event_role → party; event → case; event ← event_work_effort → work_effort; event → party_relationship (optional).	v1::ch02	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch02-2.7_2.12-2.13
Card 5 · V1 Ch 3 · Product definition, categories, identification (Figs 3.1–3.3)<br><br>What do we sell, how do we group it, and what codes is it known by?	<b>Hook:</b> one product, many shelves, many barcodes<br><b>Mantra:</b> A product sits on many dated shelves, one primary; shelves stack on shelves; party types browse shelves; codes are rows, not columns.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product</code> (what we offer, good or service via <code>product_kind</code>; not who supplies it, not stock) · <code>product_category</code> with <code>product_category_rollup</code> (many parents allowed) · <code>product_category_classification</code> (product in a category, dated, one flagged primary) · <code>market_interest</code> (which party types care about which categories) · <code>good_identification</code> (SKU, UPC, ISBN… as type + value rows).<br><br><b>W:</b> a single <code>category_id</code> column on the product (one category, no history, no second hierarchy) and a column per code (<code>sku</code>, <code>upc</code>, <code>isbn</code>: empty for most products, a schema change for every new code).<br><br><b>T:</b> the primary flag is a rule a plain FK can't hold (data-quality query). Rollup, good/service and identification are still unpractised; expect this card to be shaky.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← classification → category; category ← rollup → category (parent, child); category ← market_interest → party_type; good (= product) ← good_identification → identification_type.	v1::ch03	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch03-3.1-3.3
Card 6 · V1 Ch 3 · Product features (Fig 3.4)<br><br>What options does a product come in, which are standard, and which clash?	<b>Hook:</b> build-your-own bike<br><b>Mantra:</b> Features attach to products as required, standard, optional or selectable; features constrain features, needing or clashing, sometimes only inside one product.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product_feature</code> (a colour, size, quality, dimension…; dimensions carry a unit of measure) · <code>product_feature_applicability</code> (this feature on this product is required, standard, optional or selectable) · <code>product_feature_interaction</code> (feature A needs feature B, or A and B can't go together, optionally only within one product) · <code>unit_of_measure</code> with conversions.<br><br><b>W:</b> a column per option on the product, or a separate product row for every combination (red-small, red-large, blue-small…): an explosion of near-duplicates and no rules between them.<br><br><b>T:</b> applicability is feature ↔ <b>product</b>; interaction is feature ↔ <b>feature</b> (you mixed them up once). Null product on an interaction = it holds everywhere.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← applicability → feature; feature ← interaction → feature (twice) and → product (optional); feature → uom.	v1::ch03	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch03-3.4
Card 7 · V1 Ch 3 · Suppliers and inventory (Figs 3.5–3.6)<br><br>Who can supply it, when do we reorder, and where is the physical stock?	<b>Hook:</b> the warehouse floor<br><b>Mantra:</b> Suppliers offer products, dated; guidelines say when to reorder a good, and where; items are the physical stock in one place; variances explain the count.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>supplier_product</code> (this organization can supply this product, dated, with lead time, preference, rating) · <code>reorder_guideline</code> (for a good: reorder level and quantity, optionally per facility, geographic boundary and/or internal organization; none = everywhere) · <code>inventory_item</code> (physical stock of a good: serialized or not, in a facility <b>or</b> a container, in a status, optionally from a lot) · <code>inventory_item_variance</code> (a count found a difference, with a reason).<br><br><b>W:</b> a <code>supplier_id</code> and a <code>stock</code> column on the product: one supplier, one number for all warehouses, no lot to recall, no story of where the missing reams went.<br><br><b>T:</b> good = catalogue description, inventory item = the physical thing. The variance <i>explains</i> a change to quantity_on_hand; it doesn't replace it (the book's convention, and the qoh = balance check). Overlapping reorder scopes need a written convention.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← supplier_product → organization; good ← reorder_guideline → facility / boundary / organization; good ← inventory_item → facility | container, → lot, → status; inventory_item ← variance → reason.	v1::ch03	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch03-3.5-3.6
Card 8 · V1 Ch 3 · Pricing and costing (Figs 3.7–3.8)<br><br>What do we charge, under which conditions, and what does it cost us?	<b>Hook:</b> the price is a recipe <i>(Claude's, drafted when the figures landed)</i><br><b>Mantra:</b> Price components are conditional rules; matching ones add up to the price; cost estimates hit a product or feature, never both.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>price_component</code> (one pricing rule: base price, discount, surcharge or suggested price; for a product and/or a feature; optional "based on" conditions: region, party type, category, quantity break, order value, sale type, organization; optionally a one-time, recurring or usage charge) · <code>quantity_break</code> and <code>order_value</code> (ranges, exclusive thru) · <code>estimated_product_cost</code> (a dated estimate per cost component type, for a product <b>or</b> a feature) · <code>cost_component_type</code> (a hierarchy under materials, labour, other) · currency and time-frequency units of measure.<br><br><b>W:</b> a <code>price</code> column on the product: one number, so no discounts, regions, quantity breaks or history. One <code>cost</code> number gives no margin by materials or labour.<br><br><b>T:</b> <i>(the naive-model trap until practice gives yours)</i> looking up "the price" of a product: there isn't one. A price is computed per order line from every component whose conditions all hold.<br><br><b>D:</b> product and/or feature ← price_component → region / party type / category / quantity break / order value / sale type / organization (all optional), → currency; product | feature ← estimated_product_cost → cost_component_type.	v1::ch03	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch03-3.7-3.8
Card 9 · V1 Ch 3 · Product associations and parts (Figs 3.9–3.10)<br><br>Which products go with, replace, clash with or are built from which others?	<b>Hook:</b> the product family tree <i>(Claude's, drafted when the figures landed)</i><br><b>Mantra:</b> One dated, typed link joins product to product: component (a package or an assembly), substitute, successor, complement or clash; components explode level by level.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product_association</code> (from product → to product, dated, with a reason; what "from" and "to" mean depends on the type) · <code>product_association_type</code> (component, substitute, obsolescence, complement, incompatibility; component is 3.9b's marketing package and 3.10a's product component, one link for a kit and a BOM alike) · <code>quantity_used</code> and <code>instruction</code> for components, <code>quantity</code> for substitutes · <code>good.good_kind</code> (raw material, subassembly, finished good).<br><br><b>W:</b> columns like <code>replaced_by_product_id</code> and <code>substitute_product_id</code>: one of each, no dates, no reason, and a new column for every new kind of link.<br><br><b>T:</b> <i>(the naive-model trap until practice gives yours)</i> forgetting direction. Substitution needs a row each way; incompatibility is read both ways; a BOM explosion multiplies quantities down the levels and needs a cycle guard.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← association (from), association (to) → product; association → type; good (= product) carries good_kind. Not built: 3.10b's PART, the physical side of a product, which would take the assemblies and leave the component link only the packages.	v1::ch03	Data Models::Submodels	dm-v1-ch03-3.9-3.10
Architecture · V1 Ch 2 · People and organizations (Fig 2.14)<br><br>Everything we know about whoever we deal with, in one picture.	<b>Hook:</b> the bicycle wheel<br><b>Mantra:</b> One hub, the party: reached through mechanisms, found at facilities, heard in events that cases gather; it wears roles, and a relationship pairs two roles, never two parties.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>party</code> (the one hub: a person or an organization) · <code>party_role</code> (what it is to us: customer, supplier, employee; typed, dated) · <code>party_relationship</code> (joins two roles, from and to, dated) · <code>party_contact_mechanism</code> (a party's dated use of a shared address, phone or e-mail, with purposes) · <code>facility</code> (a physical place, nested; parties tie in through <code>facility_role</code>) · <code>communication_event</code> (what was said, with roles and purposes), gathered into a <code>communication_case</code>.<br><br><b>W:</b> every submodel answers one question about the same party. Who are they to us: a role. How are they related: a relationship between two roles. How do we reach them: a contact mechanism, through the dated link (facilities get mechanisms the same way). What did we say: an event, optionally within a relationship, gathered into a case and followed up with a work effort (Ch 6).<br><br><b>T:</b> <i>(the naive-model trap until practice gives yours)</i> a table per kind of party (customer, supplier, employee), each with its own address columns: a company that is both a customer and a supplier becomes two rows that drift apart.<br><br><b>D:</b> party → party_role ← party_relationship (from, to); party ← party_contact_mechanism → contact_mechanism (postal address ← postal_address_boundary → geographic_boundary); party ← facility_role → facility (part of) ← facility_contact_mechanism → contact_mechanism; communication_event → party_relationship and communication_case (both optional), ← communication_event_role → party. First chapter, so nothing comes in; Ch 3 borrows organization, party_type, facility and geographic_boundary.	v1::ch02 architecture	Data Models::Architecture	dm-v1-ch02-arch
Architecture · V1 Ch 3 · Products (Fig 3.11)<br><br>Everything about what we sell, from the catalogue to the shelf to the price tag.	<b>Hook:</b> the catalogue and the warehouse<br><b>Mantra:</b> Categories and features describe a product, suppliers offer it, associations link it to other products; a good is stocked as items at a facility; a price is built from components, never stored.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product</code> (the catalogue entry: what we sell) · <code>good</code> (the tangible kind of product, split by <code>good_kind</code> into raw material, subassembly, finished good) · <code>inventory_item</code> (the physical stock of a good) · <code>product_category</code> and <code>product_feature</code> (describe the product) · <code>price_component</code> and <code>estimated_product_cost</code> (build its numbers).<br><br><b>W:</b> define → describe → source → stock → price → relate. A product sits in categories (rollup, classification) that party types show market interest in; features attach through applicability, and interaction says which go together or clash. Supplier product says which organization sells it to us. Only goods get identification codes, reorder guidelines and inventory items. Price components (for a product and/or a feature) and estimated costs (a product or a feature, never both) build the numbers. Product association links product to product, the bill of materials included.<br><br><b>T:</b> <i>(the naive-model trap until practice gives yours)</i> one product table with price, cost, quantity on hand and supplier columns: one price, one supplier, no history, and no way to tell the catalogue entry from the units on the shelf.<br><br><b>D:</b> product → good (subtype); product ← product_category_classification → product_category (rollup) ← market_interest → <b>party_type</b>; product ← product_feature_applicability → product_feature (interaction); product ← supplier_product → <b>organization</b>; good ← good_identification, reorder_guideline (→ <b>facility</b> or <b>geographic_boundary</b>), inventory_item (→ <b>facility</b> | container); product and/or feature ← price_component (→ <b>geographic_boundary</b>, <b>party_type</b>, <b>organization</b>, quantity break, order value); product | feature ← estimated_product_cost; product ← product_association → product. Bold = from Ch 2. Kept: 3.10a's good subtypes, not 3.10b's PART.	v1::ch03 architecture	Data Models::Architecture	dm-v1-ch03-arch

Card 1 · Ch 2 · Parties, roles and relationships (Figs 2.1–2.7)<br><br>Who is this, what are they to us, and how are they tied to each other?	<b>Hook:</b> people wearing hats<br><b>Mantra:</b> A party is who; a role is the hat it wears; a relationship joins two hats, and a type names each hat.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>party</code> (person or organization: one supertype, a <code>party_kind</code> discriminator) · <code>party_role</code> (a party plays a <code>role_type</code>, dated) · <code>party_relationship</code> (from party role → to party role, of a <code>party_relationship_type</code> that says which role types it joins) · <code>party_classification</code> (what a party <i>is</i>, e.g. an industry or a size band, vs. what it <i>does</i> for us).<br><br><b>W:</b> the same company is a customer, a supplier and a partner at once. Naive model: separate customer and supplier tables, so the name and address are duplicated, drift, and "everyone we deal with" needs a union.<br><br><b>T:</b> the relationship joins <i>roles</i>, not parties; and the specific tables (employment, customer relationship, rollup) vs. the one generic <code>party_relationship</code> (your open gap: specific vs generic).<br><br><b>D:</b> party ← party_role → role_type; party_relationship → party_role twice; party_relationship → party_relationship_type → role_type twice.	submodel ch02
Card 2 · Ch 2 · Contact mechanisms (Figs 2.9–2.10)<br><br>How do we reach this party, for what, and since when?	<b>Hook:</b> the switchboard<br><b>Mantra:</b> Postal, phone and e-mail are one mechanism; the party's link to it owns the dates, the purposes and the "don't call"; mechanisms forward to mechanisms.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>contact_mechanism</code> (one supertype for postal address, telecom number, e-mail) · <code>party_contact_mechanism</code> (this party uses this mechanism, dated, with the non-solicitation flag, extension and optional role) · <code>party_contact_mechanism_purpose</code> (why we use that link: billing, shipping, home…, each dated) · <code>contact_mechanism_link</code> (one mechanism tied to another, e.g. call forwarding).<br><br><b>W:</b> columns like <code>home_phone</code>, <code>work_phone</code>, <code>fax</code> on the party: a fixed number of slots, no history, a shared switchboard copied onto every person, no place to say "don't call".<br><br><b>T:</b> the purpose and the do-not-solicit flag belong to the <b>link</b> (party + mechanism), not to the mechanism or the party. The same number can be fine to call for one party and off-limits for another.<br><br><b>D:</b> party ← party_contact_mechanism → contact_mechanism (→ postal / telecom / electronic); party_contact_mechanism ← purpose → purpose_type; contact_mechanism_link → contact_mechanism twice.	submodel ch02
Card 3 · Ch 2 · Communication events and case (Figs 2.7, 2.12–2.13)<br><br>Who talked to whom, how, about what, and what happened next?	<b>Hook:</b> the meeting minutes<br><b>Mantra:</b> An event happens via a medium, with many parties in roles, for purposes; a case groups past events, a work effort is the work they trigger.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>communication_event</code> (one contact, occurs via a medium, has a status, optionally in the context of a party relationship) · <code>communication_event_role</code> (any number of parties take part, each in a role: caller, receiver, attendee…) · <code>communication_event_purpose</code> (what it was about) · <code>communication_case</code> (looking back: groups the events about one issue) · <code>work_effort</code> via <code>communication_event_work_effort</code> (looking forward: the work an event triggers, many-to-many).<br><br><b>W:</b> an event tied to exactly one relationship (Fig 2.7) can't hold a three-way meeting or a call with someone we have no relationship with yet. A case alone can't say what work came out of a call.<br><br><b>T:</b> case vs work effort: the case <b>groups past events</b>; the work effort is <b>future work they cause</b> (your open gap). Also <code>valid_contact_mechanism_role</code>: a rule stored as rows.<br><br><b>D:</b> communication_event ← event_role → party; event → case; event ← event_work_effort → work_effort; event → party_relationship (optional).	submodel ch02
Card 4 · Ch 3 · Product definition, categories, identification (Figs 3.1–3.3)<br><br>What do we sell, how do we group it, and what codes is it known by?	<b>Hook:</b> one product, many shelves, many barcodes<br><b>Mantra:</b> A product sits on many dated shelves, one primary; shelves stack on shelves; party types browse shelves; codes are rows, not columns.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product</code> (what we offer, good or service via <code>product_kind</code>; not who supplies it, not stock) · <code>product_category</code> with <code>product_category_rollup</code> (many parents allowed) · <code>product_category_classification</code> (product in a category, dated, one flagged primary) · <code>market_interest</code> (which party types care about which categories) · <code>good_identification</code> (SKU, UPC, ISBN… as type + value rows).<br><br><b>W:</b> a single <code>category_id</code> column on the product (one category, no history, no second hierarchy) and a column per code (<code>sku</code>, <code>upc</code>, <code>isbn</code>: empty for most products, a schema change for every new code).<br><br><b>T:</b> the primary flag is a rule a plain FK can't hold (data-quality query). Rollup, good/service and identification are still unpractised; expect this card to be shaky.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← classification → category; category ← rollup → category (parent, child); category ← market_interest → party_type; good (= product) ← good_identification → identification_type.	submodel ch03
Card 5 · Ch 3 · Product features (Fig 3.4)<br><br>What options does a product come in, which are standard, and which clash?	<b>Hook:</b> build-your-own bike<br><b>Mantra:</b> Features attach to products as required, standard, optional or selectable; features constrain features, needing or clashing, sometimes only inside one product.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>product_feature</code> (a colour, size, quality, dimension…; dimensions carry a unit of measure) · <code>product_feature_applicability</code> (this feature on this product is required, standard, optional or selectable) · <code>product_feature_interaction</code> (feature A needs feature B, or A and B can't go together, optionally only within one product) · <code>unit_of_measure</code> with conversions.<br><br><b>W:</b> a column per option on the product, or a separate product row for every combination (red-small, red-large, blue-small…): an explosion of near-duplicates and no rules between them.<br><br><b>T:</b> applicability is feature ↔ <b>product</b>; interaction is feature ↔ <b>feature</b> (you mixed them up once). Null product on an interaction = it holds everywhere.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← applicability → feature; feature ← interaction → feature (twice) and → product (optional); feature → uom.	submodel ch03
Card 6 · Ch 3 · Suppliers and inventory (Figs 3.5–3.6)<br><br>Who can supply it, when do we reorder, and where is the physical stock?	<b>Hook:</b> the warehouse floor<br><b>Mantra:</b> Suppliers offer products, dated; guidelines say when to reorder a good, and where; items are the physical stock in one place; variances explain the count.<hr><b>S:</b> <code>supplier_product</code> (this organization can supply this product, dated, with lead time, preference, rating) · <code>reorder_guideline</code> (for a good: reorder level and quantity, optionally per facility, geographic boundary and/or internal organization; none = everywhere) · <code>inventory_item</code> (physical stock of a good: serialized or not, in a facility <b>or</b> a container, in a status, optionally from a lot) · <code>inventory_item_variance</code> (a count found a difference, with a reason).<br><br><b>W:</b> a <code>supplier_id</code> and a <code>stock</code> column on the product: one supplier, one number for all warehouses, no lot to recall, no story of where the missing reams went.<br><br><b>T:</b> good = catalogue description, inventory item = the physical thing. The variance <i>explains</i> a change to quantity_on_hand; it doesn't replace it (the book's convention, and the qoh = balance check). Overlapping reorder scopes need a written convention.<br><br><b>D:</b> product ← supplier_product → organization; good ← reorder_guideline → facility / boundary / organization; good ← inventory_item → facility | container, → lot, → status; inventory_item ← variance → reason.	submodel ch03
